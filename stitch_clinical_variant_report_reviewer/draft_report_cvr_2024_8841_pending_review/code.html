Variant Interpretation — BRCA1 c.5266dupC (p.Gln1756Profs*74) ACMG: Pathogenic

The BRCA1 c.5266dupC insertion leads to a reading frame shift resulting in a premature termination codon 74 amino acids downstream, predicted to cause loss of normal protein function either through protein truncation or nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (PVS1). This specific variant (historically designated 5382insC) represents one of the well-documented Ashkenazi Jewish founder mutations and has been extensively validated across international registries as predisposing to hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome (HBOC) (PS4, PM2_Supporting, PP5). In the context of the patient's personal diagnosis and maternal family history, this finding confirms a molecular etiology and confers substantial lifetime risks for contralateral breast malignancies and epithelial ovarian neoplasms.

Clinical Recommendations & NCCN Guideline Alignment

Per current NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment (Breast, Ovarian, and Pancreatic Version 1.2024), pathogenic variants in BRCA1 carry immediate therapeutic and prophylactic implications:

  • PARP Inhibitor Eligibility: Pathogenic germline BRCA status supports clinical evaluation for adjuvant Olaparib therapy in high-risk early HER2-negative breast cancer, or in advanced treatment settings.
  • Surgical Oncology Considerations: Increased risk of contralateral breast carcinoma (cumulative risk ~20–30% at 10 years). Consideration of bilateral mastectomy vs. enhanced surveillance is indicated.
  • Gynecologic Cancer Risk Management: Recommend risk-reducing bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (RRSO) between ages 35 and 40, or upon completion of childbearing.
  • Cascade Familial Testing: First-degree biological relatives (children, siblings, parents) have a 50% likelihood of harboring this pathogenic alteration. Targeted single-site mutation analysis is recommended.

Methodology & Regulatory Technical Limitations

Genomic DNA isolated from whole blood was enriched for target exonic regions of 48 hereditary cancer predisposition genes using hybrid capture probe chemistry. High-throughput sequencing was conducted on Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platforms (paired-end 150bp). Minimum 99.4% of targeted bases achieved ≥100x depth. Variants were annotated using Human Genome Variation Society (HGVS v20.05) nomenclature and classified following ACMG/AMP standards. Technical limitations: Deep intronic single-nucleotide variants outside canonical ±20bp splice boundaries, large chromosomal inversions, and low-level mosaicism (<10%) may not be reliably identified.
